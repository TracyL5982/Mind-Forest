01 · Product vision

A forest of projects. A place for every thought.

Mind Palace is a spatial workspace for capturing, recovering, and developing ideas with an AI librarian. Its central promise is continuity: return to a project and recognize the place you left.

The forest is the overview of the user’s projects. Each project appears as a tree or stack of contents, in its remembered position. Entering a project reveals its own saved arrangement of notes, images, sources, questions, and smaller collections. Opening those collections reveals further layers. The same principle applies at every scale: the user decides where things belong, and the space remembers.

Spatial organization is part of the thinking process. People can make piles, spread sources for comparison, arrange a sequence, or develop branches. The librarian helps gather and interpret material within that space. Its contributions appear beside relevant content, where the user can inspect, keep, remove, edit, or reposition them.

Core promise

Returning restores your place. The librarian contributes without disturbing your arrangement.

This is a product vision, not a description of shipped behavior. Core vision identifies the user’s proposed experience. Proposed refinement identifies a recommendation to validate before treating it as a settled specification.

02 · Spatial principles

Stable places, flexible structures

Core vision

Every layer remembers

Projects retain their places in the forest. Contents retain their places within projects and collections. Users can change these arrangements themselves, or ask the librarian to reorganize them according to stated criteria.

Core vision

Growth has structure

Collections can expand into further stacks and branches. A branch’s children open toward the foreground; earlier material remains farther back. Users can inspect a thread’s full lineage.

Core vision

Organization stays personal

A project does not require one universal arrangement. Different collections can use stacks, grids, sequences, or loose groups according to how the user thinks about them.

Refinement: distinguish three transformations

The original concept includes flattening/collapsing stacks into a view where stronger connections produce greater proximity. The recommended interaction separates its effects:

TransformationBehaviorWhat stays stable
CollapseFold descendants into their representative stack.The stack’s anchor and surrounding contents.
FlattenExpose descendants together for inspection.The saved arrangement remains recoverable.
Connections viewArrange material by relationships and proximity.The original project layout; adoption is a separate choice.

Children coming forward is a proposed local default, rather than a rule that forces every descendant ever closer to the viewer. Entering a deeper thread changes the working context. Lineage remains available after manual rearrangement. The visible tree can also contain cross-links: belonging to a collection, developing from an idea, and supporting a claim are distinct relationships.

03 · Starting and returning

Four ways a project begins

Starting pointCore experience
Capture somethingDrop content into the palace. Follow an explicit destination or a strong match to an existing project. Otherwise, the capture becomes the starting point of a new project.
Describe an intentionAsk to start a project on a subject or goal. The librarian curates an initial set of material in response.
Start from existing ideasSelect one or more items across projects and use them to begin a new one.
Receive a projectOpen a project shared by a friend and develop it within the agreed sharing model.

Proposed refinement An unmatched capture can begin as a lightweight seed, so saving a fragment does not require establishing a fully developed project. Confident filing can remain automatic, with an understandable destination and undo.

For cross-project reuse, distinguish Use here (another appearance of the same memory), Move here (change its membership or location), and Make an independent copy. Shared projects likewise need an explicit choice between an independent import and a live shared space.

A concrete session

  1. Return. Open “Interface research” in its familiar position. Its collections and viewing context are restored.
  2. Notice. Read a source and highlight a passage about occlusion. Attach a question for the librarian.
  3. Continue elsewhere. Leave the project while the requested investigation runs, according to the project’s work preference.
  4. Find an addition. Return to the same arrangement. An attached result bundle marks the new answer and suggested sources.
  5. Develop. Keep one source, compare passages, and branch into an alternative interface direction.
  6. Revisit. Inspect Connections view, return to the original arrangement, and save the developed thread with its question and conclusion.

04 · Working with content

Objects become handles for inquiry

Select the material you mean, then act on it. Conversation remains available throughout the workspace.

ActionExpected outcome
Highlight / annotateKeep a passage, comment, note, or question attached to its source.
Ask librarianInvestigate the selected material and attach an answer, supporting passages, and unresolved questions.
BranchDevelop a new direction as a child thread, with its origin and lineage preserved.
ExpandFind and curate relevant material into a nearby suggestion bundle.
ConverseCreate, edit, compare, or move content through a live exchange grounded in the current selection and project.
Arrange / edit / removeManipulate the workspace directly, with undo and a clear distinction between removing an appearance and deleting the memory.

Proposed refinement Give each action a predictable default output. An answer stays attached to its question; an expansion supplies candidates; a branch creates a direction to develop. The librarian may suggest another structure, and the user can request one conversationally.

A personal question is not necessarily an instruction to an agent. The user can address the librarian explicitly or enable a project convention for actionable annotations. This controls what initiates work; real-time or background preferences control when it happens.

05 · The librarian’s contribution

Useful additions, visible boundaries

Core boundary

The librarian can place its suggested contents. It cannot move established contents unless the user requests rearrangement or organization.

Suggestions appear near the content that prompted them and receive a distinct treatment. Users decide whether to keep, remove, or move them. Explicit requests authorize the corresponding work within their scope; the user should not have to approve every routine step.

Proposed refinements Hold suggestions steady once presented. Once the user deliberately places, edits, pins, or keeps one, protect that placement like other established content. Use a nearby bundle or designated free area so new arrivals do not push existing material aside. Keep excess results collapsed.

Separate pending status from origin. An article recommended by AI is externally authored; a generated explanation is AI-authored. Keeping either clears its pending treatment without erasing its source. Pair color with a readable label or another visual cue.

Background work returns to the originating thread with a small new-results marker. It respects the user’s current edits and does not trigger an uncontrolled chain of further work. Important claims lead to original sources, and the librarian makes the material it actually examined inspectable.

Try the annotation contract · proposed refinement

“I wonder whether depth helps me remember this.”

Saved as your annotation. It stays attached to this memory. The librarian does not start work.

Connection to the agent architecture

One visible librarian coordinates specialized workflows and project context behind the scenes. Memories can have several spatial appearances while sharing one underlying identity. Project continuity, source evidence, user preferences, and undo support the experience; they do not require exposing an agent organizational chart to the user.

06 · Decisions to validate

Keep the promise precise

These choices remain open. The interactive study illustrates possible behavior without settling them.

  • Entry and depth. What restores on return: entry direction, focus, open stacks, and active view? How does a very deep branch open comfortably?
  • Actionable annotations. Which project conventions distinguish notes-to-self from requests? When should the librarian work immediately or after departure?
  • Suggestion lifecycle. How many candidates arrive at once, how are dismissals remembered, and how long do unkept suggestions remain available?
  • Connections view. Which relationships determine proximity? How does the view explain why objects are near each other?
  • Sharing. Does the first version support imported snapshots or live collaboration? Which edits affect other appearances of a memory?

07 · First product slice

Prove one complete thinking session

Build the return → question → background answer → keep → branch → map → return loop using a small collection of real notes. Validate the same content on desktop and one target headset before broadening the spatial interaction vocabulary.

Proposed acceptance criteria

  • Projects, placements, and expansion state survive leaving and restarting.
  • Adding suggestions and opening other views leave saved positions intact.
  • Users can identify pending suggestions, inspect their sources, and keep one without an unexpected jump.
  • A question has a visible work state and returns a bounded answer attached to the correct source.
  • A branch preserves its origin; a memory can appear in another project without becoming an accidental duplicate.
  • Collapse, Connections view, and undo have predictable effects; returning recovers the earlier arrangement.

Evaluate whether people can find a remembered source, compare relevant passages, explain what changed, and resume their reasoning later. The visual concept succeeds when its spatial behavior helps those tasks.

Space, layout, and returning to a thought

  • Space to ThinkCHI 2010

    Spatial arrangement as external memory and a meaningful organizational layer. Motivates leaving material available for comparison; does not isolate depth as the cause of benefit.

  • Immersive Space to Think2020 thesis · qualitative study

    Observed surrounding document arrangements and depth used for relevance. A precedent for working sets in space, with navigation and occlusion tradeoffs.

  • Maps Around MeISS 2020 · project & videos

    Pull detailed child views out beside an overview while preserving context. Borrow the linked overview/detail interaction; map data has spatial properties that notes do not.

  • LitForagerISMAR / TVCG 2025 · project & video

    Combines literature discovery, summaries, clustering, annotations, gestures, and voice in a 3D workspace. Useful for the Explore loop; an observational study, not a 3D-superiority result.

  • Effects of Display Layout on Spatial MemoryISS 2022 · controlled VR studies

    Flat layouts outperformed full circles on spatial-pattern recall; semicircles were preferred and showed no significant performance difference from flat. A reason to test a forward-facing working area.

  • Uncovering Best Practices in Immersive Space to ThinkISMAR 2023

    Analysis quality did not differ significantly between broad layout categories. Iteration, note creation, and document spread were associated with quality; these correlations are not causal guarantees.

  • Data MountainUIST 1998

    User-positioned document thumbnails and spatial recall. Its comparison with browser Favorites supports investigating recognizable places, without isolating a general advantage of 3D.

  • Pad++CHI 1995 · interface technique

    Semantic zoom changes representation with scale. Informs the progression from project or stack overview to readable content while maintaining navigational context.

Working with AI through shared objects

  • CocoaCHI 2026

    Editable, executable plans interleave human and agent work. Informs a research trail whose steps and intermediate outputs can be revised together.

  • SynergiUIST 2023 · project & demo

    Seed a research thread from highlighted text, then edit AI-expanded, evidence-linked threads. A precedent for developing selected memories into a revisable structure.

  • DynaVisCHI 2024

    A request produces both an edit and persistent controls for further adjustment. Suggests asking once, then steering directly; accumulating controls can overwhelm.

  • SpellburstUIST 2023 · project & video

    Branching, comparison, merging, and semantic sliders support creative-code exploration. Informs parallel interpretations of a constellation without overwriting its current version.

  • IntentTaggerCHI 2025

    Editable intent tags expose small parts of a creative brief. Suggests inspectable scope and constraints attached to a thinking session.

  • DirectGPTCHI 2024 · project & demo

    Selection and direct actions scope transformations. A precedent for selecting memories or passages to communicate what a request refers to.

  • ABRAEMNLP 2026 system demonstration · project & video

    Agents publish task-specific meanings for actions on shared views. Relevant to readable interaction contracts; its evaluation concerns agent capabilities rather than demonstrated human benefit.

  • GraphologueUIST 2023

    Linked text and diagrams make concepts handles for follow-up inquiry. Generated diagrams still need source grounding before their edges become factual claims.

  • Magentic-UIMicrosoft Research 2025 · experimental system

    Co-planning, taking over a shared task surface, and resuming agent work inform live collaboration beyond receiving a final answer.
