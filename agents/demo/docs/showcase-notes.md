# The showcase trees: what their makers were unsure of

Four agents wrote the showcase trees on 2026-11-16, one each. Every paper's title, authors, year and journal were confirmed with Crossref, and every found picture's licence was read from its own record. These are the details they could not confirm.

## Constellations

- `lombardi-circle` (a pin): credited to Mark Lombardi on the strength of Pinterest's title alone; the card says so.
- `euler-bridges`: the date 1736 comes from the Commons record of another figure of the same paper.
- `bayer-orion`: dated 1661 as its Commons page says; the atlas is usually dated 1603, so this is probably a later printing.
- `dunhuang-chart`: the photographer's scale bar was cropped off; the holder is given as Wikimedia Commons, with credit to the International Dunhuang Project.
- `literati-screen`: The Met's title is a character too long for a card, so it reads "Paintings and Calligraphy by Iga Ueno Literati".
- `tube-map-1908`: Commons names no maker, so the card has none.
- `uele-drainage`: the picture was confirmed through NASA's API; the address of its page follows NASA's usual pattern and was not opened.
- Left out on purpose: two pins that show real people's faces.

## Seed spirals

- `vogel-1979`: no open source gives the paper's abstract; the card's words follow a description of his formula.
- `golden-angle`: the 137.5 degrees comes from Wikipedia's article, not from the picture's own page.
- `douady-couder-1992`: the card follows the abstract as OpenAlex gives it.
- `blossfeldt-fern`: dated only by the 1935 annual it was printed in.
- `sand-patterns` (a pin): it carries a faint stock-library watermark, and "sand on a ringing plate" is a reading of the picture.
- `chladni-figures`: a modern redrawing of Chladni's figures, listed as public domain.
- `spiral-aloe`, `six-spirals`, `curled-leaf` (pins): under 300 px wide, the largest Pinterest serves.

## Imaginary planet

- `kiang-2007`, `ehlers-2014`: Crossref holds no abstract; the one-line summaries are from the agent's own knowledge of the papers.
- `bierstadt-vesuvius`: "a moon of volcanoes", said of Io, comes from another NASA record than the picture's own.
- `nasmyth-lunar-crater`: the record gives only title, maker, 1874 and woodburytype; the book's edge and colour bar were cropped off.
- `morghen-pumpkin-houses`, `morghen-moon-carriage`, `loewy-puiseux-moon`: titles shortened to fit a card; the museum lists the two Morghen prints as "possibly after" Hoüel, and the cards name Morghen only.
- `orange-canyon`, `blue-giants`, `lily-pads-from-below` (pins): a maker's name or stamp is printed in the picture; the cards name nobody.

## Paper architecture

- `cobweb-valentine`: the picture is the Met record's second view (the web lifted by its thread), of the same object and page.
- `overvelde-2017`, `prism-diagram`: the paper hangs by the pin because the diagram's printed labels match the paper's title; it was not compared with the journal's figure, and the card's sentence is from the agent's own knowledge.
- `giegher-pleats`: probably folded napkins, which the Met record does not say, so the card does not either.
- `miura-crease-pattern`: "two kinds of line mark the two directions of fold" is the usual reading of a crease pattern, not a statement of its page.
- `durer-spiral`: the description of the page is a reading of the picture; the record gives only the book's title and date.
- `note-curved-crease`, `note-slot`: notes written by the agent from general knowledge.
- The Met's pictures are its 600 px web size, smaller than most pins.
